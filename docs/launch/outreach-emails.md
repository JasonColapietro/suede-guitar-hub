# GuitarHub cold outreach templates

**Last updated:** 2026-11-10
**Send from:** a real, monitored address. Replies to these go to a person.

Five templates, five different angles, five different asks. Each is under 150
words on purpose: a cold email that needs scrolling gets archived.

## Rules for using these

1. **Fill every slot.** Anything in `[SQUARE BRACKETS]` is a slot. An unfilled
   slot is worse than no email, and a slot filled with a generic compliment is
   the same thing in a nicer font. The slot marked `[SPECIFIC]` must contain a
   detail you could only know by consuming their work: a timestamp, a sentence
   they wrote, a decision they made and explained.
2. **No invented connection.** Do not say "a friend recommended you", "we met
   at", or "I saw you're connected to". If there is a real mutual, name them and
   check with them first.
3. **Only real urgency.** The one date is the price change: "Launch price $79.
   Goes to $99 on December 1, 2026." State it once, as written, and only before
   that date. No limited spots, no countdowns, no "usually $99".
4. **Lead with the promise and the free start.** "Play real chords and improvise
   your first solo in 30 days." A placement opens one module free; the tuner,
   metronome, diagnostic and tempo ladder are free with no account. Lifetime
   access is $79 one time, with a 90-day money-back guarantee on web purchases.
   Microphone exercises score single-note pitch and timing. Quote no student
   results until they exist.
5. **One follow-up, maximum.** Seven days later, three sentences, and then stop.
6. **A no ends it.** Do not re-pitch a different angle to someone who declined.

**Send these only after `README.md` Step 0 and Step 1 pass.** Cold outreach is
one-shot: a creator, teacher, editor or podcaster who hits a broken page or a
missing plan email does not open the second email.

Links used in these templates: `https://guitarhub.org`, `/breakthrough` (the
free 30-day plan), `/start` (the free module), `/diagnose`, `/tempo`,
`https://strumly.suedeai.ai/guides`, `https://social.suedeai.ai`,
`https://suedeai.ai`.

## 1. Guitar-education creator (YouTube, TikTok, Instagram)

**Angle:** a free tool with no signup wall is something they can put on screen
without sending their audience into a funnel.
**Ask:** find one thing that is wrong with the diagnostic.

> **Subject:** A free practice tool with no signup, if you want to tear it apart
>
> Hi [FIRST NAME],
>
> I build guitar practice tools at GuitarHub. I am writing because of
> [SPECIFIC: the video or moment, and the point they made in it].
>
> We have a practice plateau diagnostic that ends by naming the thing that
> stopped producing change: https://guitarhub.org/diagnose. No account, no
> email gate, nothing stored on our side. It runs in the browser and keeps your
> answers in that browser's own storage, which you can clear from the page.
>
> The ask is not a mention. Would you run it once, as one of your own students
> would, and tell me the single question that is wrong or missing? You have
> diagnosed more stuck players than I have, and I would rather hear it now than
> after a thousand people use it.
>
> Either way, thanks for [SPECIFIC, one clause].
>
> [YOUR NAME]
> guitarhub.org

>
> [YOUR NAME]

## 3. Music-education blog, guest piece

**Angle:** a specific argument, offered on spec, so the editor risks nothing.
**Ask:** permission to send a finished draft they are free to reject.
**Sender:** founder voice. "I built the practice tools" is only true if Jason
sends it. Anyone else has to rewrite that sentence.

> **Subject:** Guest piece on spec: [WORKING TITLE]
>
> Hi [FIRST NAME],
>
> [SPECIFIC: the post on their site you read, and the claim in it you are
> extending or disagreeing with.]
>
> I would like to write [WORKING TITLE, e.g. "Why the 30-day challenge format
> fails, and the one change that fixes it"] for [PUBLICATION]. The argument:
> most practice advice cannot be checked, so players cannot tell a good month
> from a bad one. A recording taken before and after can. The piece would be
> about [WORD COUNT] words, with no product pitch in the body.
>
> I will write it on spec. You publish it or you do not, and there is no
> invoice either way.
>
> Background: I built https://guitarhub.org and write the guides there, so you
> can judge the writing before you answer.
>
> [YOUR NAME]

## 4. Guitar teacher

**Angle:** the tempo ladder is the thing teachers write on a napkin every week.
This does it in thirty seconds and costs nothing.
**Ask:** use it with one student, then tell us what broke.

> **Subject:** Free tempo ladder builder, and a question about your students
>
> Hi [FIRST NAME],
>
> You teach [SPECIFIC: instrument, level, setting, or a detail from their site].
>
> GuitarHub has a free tempo ladder builder: give it one difficult passage and
> it returns a starting speed, a target, and the steps between them.
> https://guitarhub.org/tempo. No account, no student data collected, nothing
> stored on our servers. You can put it on screen in a lesson or send the link.
>
> Would you use it with one student this week and tell me what broke? Where the
> steps were wrong, where a student ignored it, where it assumed something your
> students do not have.
>
> The tempo ladder is free, and there is no teacher program to join. I want the
> tool to survive contact with a real lesson.
>
> [YOUR NAME]
> guitarhub.org

## 5. Podcast pitch

**Angle:** one arguable position, not a founder story.
**Ask:** yes or no on an episode, with the questions supplied up front.
**Sender:** founder voice, for the same reason as template 3.

> **Subject:** Episode idea for [SHOW]: practice you can actually check
>
> Hi [FIRST NAME],
>
> [SPECIFIC: the episode you listened to, the guest, and the exchange that
> prompted this.]
>
> I am [YOUR NAME]. I built GuitarHub, a guitar course built around playing
> real chords and improvising a first solo in 30 days, and Strumly, an AI
> guitar coach, at Suede AI.
>
> The position I would argue on [SHOW]: guitar education sells lessons and
> almost never sells a finish line, which is why players stall with a hundred
> half-learned songs. The fix is a fixed order and a 30-day finish: chords,
> then one scale, then four bars of your own, with a recording at each end.
>
> Would a [LENGTH] episode on that work for you? I can send three questions I
> would want asked, including the hardest one you could put to it.
>
> [YOUR NAME]
> guitarhub.org
